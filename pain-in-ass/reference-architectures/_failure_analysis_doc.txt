Autonomous Multi-Agent Trading System

Alpaca AI Trading Agents Hackathon — Project Summary & Failure Analysis

Prepared ahead of Monday live trading launch  |  Paper trading, $100,000 account, options + equities

1. What We Are Building

An autonomous, multi-agent AI trading system built on Alpaca's Trading API, MCP server, and CLI, operating on a dedicated paper account with a $100,000 starting budget across equities and options. The system moves market data through a graph-based machine learning pipeline, then hands signals to a multi-agent decision layer that debates, sizes, and executes trades without manual intervention.

1.1 Core Data & Modeling Pipeline

The signal-generation path, in sequence:

Alpaca market data ingestion, persisted to SQLite

Topology Builder — constructs the relationship structure between tradable assets

Financial Graph — nodes are assets, edges represent sector, correlation, and supply-chain relationships

GATv2 (Graph Attention Network v2) — learns attention-weighted importance across asset relationships

Attention + Graph Embeddings — produces an interpretable, relationship-aware feature representation

XGBoost + GATv2 hybrid — combines embeddings with gradient-boosted prediction for the trading signal

News-augmented extension (in progress for Monday) — historical and weekend news encoded as node/edge features feeding back into the GATv2 layer, so the graph reflects sentiment and event context, not just price correlation

1.2 Agentic Decision Layer

Signals from the graph/ML pipeline feed a multi-agent orchestration layer (already built across all phases) where specialized agents — bull case, bear case, and risk — evaluate each proposed trade independently before a position is opened. Agent-to-agent (A2A) communication is logged, including proposals, confidence levels, dissent, and the final adjudicated decision. A dynamic topology controller is intended to let the system adjust which relationships and which agents matter most as market conditions shift.

1.3 Execution Plan

Markets are closed Friday through Sunday. That window is being used to run news through the pipeline and stage orders, with live execution and re-validation happening at Monday's open.

2. Failure Point Analysis

The categories below cover where a system like this typically breaks — in backtesting validity, in the modeling pipeline, in multi-agent coordination, in options mechanics, in live execution, and in how it's judged. Treat this as a punch list to close out before Monday, not a list of things that are necessarily currently wrong.

2.1 Data & Backtesting Integrity

Failure Point

Why It Happens

Impact If Unaddressed

Close-Out Action

Look-ahead bias in graph construction

Building the Financial Graph using the full historical dataset at once lets correlations from the future leak into past decision points.

Backtest returns look far better than live performance will — the single most common way a strong-looking hackathon demo falls apart under questioning.

Rebuild the graph on a rolling/walk-forward basis, using only data available as of each decision timestamp. Re-run the backtest this way before Monday.

News timing leakage

Weekend news encoded into node/edge features for historical backtesting may be dated after the price move it's meant to explain.

The news-augmented GATv2 will appear predictive in backtest but won't replicate live.

Timestamp-align news strictly to publish time; when backtesting, mask any news published after the decision point for that day.

Survivorship bias in the trading universe

Testing only on currently-listed, currently-liquid assets ignores delisted or halted names from the backtest period.

Overstates historical performance slightly but consistently.

Note the limitation explicitly in the write-up if not corrected — transparency here reads well to technical judges.

Transaction costs and slippage not modeled

Paper fills are often idealized; backtests frequently assume zero friction.

Strategy edge may be smaller — or negative — once realistic costs are applied, especially with options bid/ask spreads.

Apply a conservative slippage and commission assumption in the backtest and disclose it in the metrics.

2.2 Modeling Pipeline (GATv2 / XGBoost)

Failure Point

Why It Happens

Impact If Unaddressed

Close-Out Action

Overfitting on a short history

GNN + gradient-boosted hybrids have many parameters relative to the amount of clean historical data available.

Strong backtest, weak or random live performance over the 5-day judged window.

Hold out a final validation slice never touched during tuning; report metrics only from that slice.

Attention weight instability

GATv2 attention weights can shift sharply with small input changes, especially with a thin graph (few nodes/edges).

Explanations of 'why' a trade was made become unreliable right when you need them most for the demo.

Sanity-check attention weights across consecutive days for the same assets; flag and smooth abrupt swings rather than trusting them blindly.

Cold-start on new or newly-connected nodes

Assets with limited history or newly added edges have weak embeddings.

Signal quality degrades unevenly across the portfolio without an obvious cause.

Apply a minimum-history filter before an asset is eligible for a live signal.

News embedding pipeline unfinished by Monday

It's being built over a single weekend under time pressure.

Risk of a half-working feature causing worse decisions than not having it at all.

Feature-flag it — keep the pre-news pipeline as the default execution path, and only promote the news-augmented path once its walk-forward backtest clears your existing baseline.

2.3 Multi-Agent Orchestration

Failure Point

Why It Happens

Impact If Unaddressed

Close-Out Action

Agent deadlock or contradictory signals

Bull, bear, and risk agents can disagree with no clear resolution rule.

Missed trades, stalled decisions, or inconsistent behavior that's hard to explain live in the demo.

Define and log an explicit tie-break rule (e.g., risk agent has veto power; ties default to no-trade).

Non-determinism across runs

LLM-based agent reasoning can produce different outputs on identical inputs.

Backtest results aren't reproducible, which undermines credibility with judges who ask you to re-run it.

Fix random seeds where possible; log full agent inputs/outputs so any run can be explained even if not bit-for-bit reproducible.

Latency in agent-to-agent exchange

Sequential multi-agent debate takes real wall-clock time before an order is placed.

At market open, slower decisions can mean worse fills during the most volatile minutes of the day.

Time-box the debate loop; have a fallback single-agent fast path for high-volatility opens.

Silent error propagation

If one agent fails or times out, the orchestration layer may proceed with incomplete input.

A bad or missing signal gets treated as a real one and acted on.

Require explicit confirmation from every agent before a trade is allowed to execute; fail closed, not open.

2.4 Options Mechanics

Failure Point

Why It Happens

Impact If Unaddressed

Close-Out Action

Assignment and expiration handling

Options behavior (assignment, exercise, expiration) is more complex than equities and easy to under-model.

Positions can behave unexpectedly right when you're not watching, over the weekend or mid-week.

Explicitly check expiration dates against your 5-day window before Monday; avoid same-week expiries unless intentional.

Wide bid/ask spreads on paper fills

Less liquid options chains can show unrealistic paper fills at the midpoint.

P&L looks better on paper than a live options strategy would realistically achieve.

Restrict the options universe to liquid, high-open-interest names/strikes.

Greeks not factored into position sizing

A signal-driven system optimized for equities may size options positions without accounting for delta/theta/vega exposure.

Effective risk is larger than intended, especially through a weekend gap.

Have the risk agent explicitly check greeks-based exposure limits, not just notional position size.

2.5 Live Execution (Monday Open)

Failure Point

Why It Happens

Impact If Unaddressed

Close-Out Action

Weekend gap risk

Orders staged from Friday/weekend news analysis are based on stale prices once Monday opens.

Pre-staged orders can execute at prices that no longer make sense given the actual open.

Re-validate every staged order against Monday's actual open price and volatility before firing — treat staging as a draft, not a commitment.

API rate limits / order rejections

Bursty order submission at open, especially across multiple agents, can hit Alpaca API limits or get rejected.

Missed entries or partial execution of the intended strategy right when it matters most for the demo.

Add retry-with-backoff and explicit handling for rejected/partial orders, with a log entry either way.

Single point of failure in SQLite under concurrent agent writes

Multiple agents writing state simultaneously can hit database locking issues.

A crash or silent write failure mid-trading-day, with no clear record of what happened.

Confirm write-ahead logging (WAL) mode is enabled, or move to a queue in front of the DB for the live window.

No kill switch

Fully autonomous systems need a fast manual override if something behaves unexpectedly live.

A single bad signal compounding across multiple trades before anyone notices.

Build and test a manual pause/halt-all-trading control before Monday morning.

2.6 Judging & Presentation Risk

Failure Point

Why It Happens

Impact If Unaddressed

Close-Out Action

5 days is not statistically meaningful

A short live window can show a good or bad number purely from variance, not skill.

Judges who understand markets may discount a raw P&L number on its own.

Lead with risk-adjusted metrics and the benchmark comparison (vs. SPY), not the raw dollar P&L, in the pitch.

Complexity outpaces the demo's ability to explain it

Six pipeline stages plus multi-agent orchestration plus a news layer is a lot to walk through in a short demo slot.

Judges leave impressed by ambition but unclear on what actually happened and why it worked.

Script the demo around 2-3 concrete trades end-to-end (signal → agent debate → decision → outcome), not a full architecture walkthrough.

Unfinished pieces presented as finished

The news-augmented GATv2 layer is still being built this weekend under time pressure.

A question that pokes at the newest, least-tested part of the system is the most likely place a technical judge finds a gap.

Be ready to clearly separate 'validated and live' from 'built but not yet fully validated' in the pitch — judges respond well to honest scoping.

3. Pre-Monday Close-Out Checklist

Backtest, walk-forward, not single-pass — rebuild the graph rolling forward through time, no full-history leakage.

Benchmark every return figure against SPY/QQQ over the identical window.

Report Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown, and win rate alongside raw P&L, not instead of it.

News feature: feature-flagged, walk-forward validated, and defaults off if it doesn't beat the existing baseline by Sunday night.

A2A decision logs capture proposal, confidence, dissent, and final call for every trade — this is your demo script.

Explicit agent tie-break rule documented and tested (who wins on disagreement).

Options universe restricted to liquid names/strikes; expirations checked against the 5-day window.

Risk agent checks greeks exposure, not just notional position size.

Monday-open re-validation step confirmed to run before any pre-staged order fires.

Kill switch built and tested before live trading starts.

Demo script drafted around 2-3 real trades end-to-end, with an honest note on what's fully validated vs. newly added.

4. Updated Scoring Guidance (from hackathon admin)

Clarified scoring approach as of the admin's most recent message — this supersedes the benchmark-comparison assumption in earlier drafts of this document.

Performance is judged on total account equity during the official scoring window, not on absolute P&L in isolation.

Risk-adjusted metrics (Sharpe, Sortino) are not explicitly part of scoring — track them internally for tuning and as backup answers, but don't lead the pitch with them.

No benchmark comparison (e.g., vs. SPY) is indicated as part of scoring — drop that framing from the demo.

Creativity, autonomy, and robustness of the trading workflow are explicit judged criteria alongside equity performance.

4.1 What This Changes

Equity curve shape matters more than a single end number. "Total equity during the window" suggests continuous tracking, not just start/end — a steady, sustained curve should read better than a high-variance spike-then-giveback pattern, even at similar final totals.

Lead the demo with creativity, autonomy, and robustness, not with risk metrics. The GATv2 + XGBoost + news-augmented graph pipeline is the creativity story; the multi-agent A2A debate loop is the autonomy story; the failure-mode close-out list (Section 2 and 3 above) is the robustness story.

No need to over-optimize for risk-adjusted purity. Without explicit Sharpe/Sortino scoring, a big drawdown still directly hurts the equity number with no offsetting credit — so the target stays steady, explainable equity growth, not a high-variance swing for a large number.

5. Positioning Note: Conservative Strategy ("Beleth" Reference Point)

A participant reported their agent, Beleth, closed day one with a slim ~$373 profit (0.37% on $100k) on a deliberately cautious, conservative options approach. This is a useful reference point for calibrating posture, not a number to target directly — a single day's result from another team reflects their specific sizing, options structure, and market luck, none of which is reproducible on demand.

5.1 What Is Actually Controllable

Small position sizing relative to account value per trade

Defined-risk options structures (covered calls, cash-secured puts, capped spreads) over naked directional bets

Tight risk-agent limits on notional exposure and greeks (delta/theta/vega), not just position count

Trading on higher-confidence signals only, rather than firing on every marginal GATv2/XGBoost output — fewer, better trades reduce exposure to slippage and spread costs

If the risk agent's sizing and greeks limits are already tight, a slim-but-positive day is a plausible and expected outcome of the existing architecture — it does not require a different model. The priority is confirming those limits are enforced tightly enough in practice, not chasing a specific competitor's number.
